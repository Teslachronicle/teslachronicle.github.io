Cybercab opens to the public in Dallas as Tesla turns autonomy into a service

The Elon Times · Wednesday, November 25, 2026 · A ledger story

In one week Tesla started public two-seat Cybercab rides in a second city, began rolling out FSD v15 toward a December wide release, and attached a required $299 monthly subscription to personal Cybercab orders — while Italy approved Assisted Driving and the EU committee meeting slipped to December 15.

On November 19, Tesla’s Robotaxi account posted, “Howdy Dallas. Cybercab has arrived in your city,” and opened public Cybercab rides there through the Tesla Robotaxi app. Dallas is the second city, after Austin, to get the two-seat Cybercab with no steering wheel or pedals. Robotaxi in Dallas has already run Model Y vehicles unsupervised since April 18. The November launch is the one Tesla stated on its October 21 earnings call. (@robotaxi, Nov. 19, 2026; @Tesla, Nov. 19, 2026)

The authorized list in Texas grew with it. A TeslaNorth check of Texas Department of Motor Vehicles records on November 24 counted 822 Cybercabs and 734 Model Ys — 1,556 in all, up from 1,051 on October 27. The registry counts authorized vehicles, not ones carrying riders. As of November 11, Tesla said the service runs 24 hours a day in every city it serves. Bay Area rides remain supervised. (Texas DMV; TeslaNorth, Nov. 25, 2026)

Chronicle’s read: A public ride in a second city is a real step. An authorized roster is still a permission list. Dallas already had unsupervised Model Y robotaxis; what arrived this week is the purpose-built, two-seat car.

A car you can order, and a fee it requires

Tesla is also trying to sell the car, not only offer the ride. On November 13 it said select customers are being invited to buy a Cybercab for personal use, priced at $29,990 plus a required $299-a-month Autonomy Subscription, with estimated delivery in December 2026. Buyers must take delivery in Texas, and the car runs unsupervised only inside Tesla’s Robotaxi service areas. Musk said the fee is too high and should be closer to about $115 a month. No customer-owned Cybercab had been delivered as of November 16. Buyer screenshots reported by Teslarati say the car “will not operate without” the subscription; those details are reported from screenshots, not a public Tesla page. (TechCrunch, Nov. 14, 2026; Teslarati, November 2026)

The factory side is being cleared for the same vehicle. Musk said Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around mid-2027 to make room for Cybercab, a target later moved through the end of summer 2027. By November 24–25, Tesla had raised the Cyberbeast, the only U.S. trim still orderable, to $114,990, from $109,990, and stopped offering leases on new orders. On November 16, Tesla told Reuters it would invest €3 billion at Giga Berlin aimed at up to 1 million Cybercabs a year, with production in 2029. (Not a Tesla App, Nov. 25, 2026; Reuters, Nov. 16, 2026)

Chronicle’s read: The ride, the invitation and the factory plan are three different things. Public rides in Dallas are running. Personal orders are invitations, not deliveries, and they come with a monthly fee Musk himself has called too high.

The software heads for December

On November 21 at 11:24 a.m. PT, @Tesla posted that FSD v15 was starting to roll out, “upgrading every part of the end-to-end driving network.” Tesla cited “25x better handling of safety critical cases,” its own claim, and a new Tesla Reality View that visualizes what FSD is planning. Ashok Elluswamy, who leads AI at Tesla, said it was “releasing to the first wave of customers today.” The night before, Musk had written, “FSD V15 goes to wide release next month.” From that November 20 post, the wide release would be December 2026. Tesla’s Super Intelligence account said v15 includes an “Upgraded vision encoder shared between FSD, Robotaxi, and Optimus.” (@Tesla, Nov. 21, 2026; @aelluswamy, Nov. 21, 2026; @elonmusk, Nov. 20, 2026; @TeslaSI, Nov. 21, 2026)

Chronicle’s read: The first wave is not the wide release. The shared encoder is a shared seeing model, not a claim that customer cars and Cybercab now run one identical driving stack.

Italy says yes. Brussels moves the date.

On November 20, Italy’s Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport granted provisional type approval to Tesla Assisted Driving, the supervised FSD renamed for Europe. Transport Minister Matteo Salvini announced it, and Tesla Europe posted, “Now approved in Italy, rollout begins soon,” without a date. Italy is the 12th EU country to allow the system and the first of the bloc’s large markets. Customer rollout had not started as of November 20. (@teslaeurope, Nov. 20, 2026; @matteosalvinimi, Nov. 20, 2026)

The EU-wide vote is still ahead, and the date slipped. A European Commission spokesperson told Reuters the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles meeting planned for December 1 has moved to December 15. No reason was given. The committee has discussed Tesla’s file at two meetings without a vote. There is still no EU-wide approval, and active driver supervision remains required where the system is already in use. (Electrek, citing Reuters, Nov. 25, 2026)

At the New York Times DealBook Summit on November 18, Musk said Tesla plans an autonomous Model Y without a steering wheel or pedals, meant to carry groups of six or seven alongside the two-seat Cybercab. He gave no launch date, price or factory, and did not say whether it would be sold or kept for the Robotaxi fleet. (Electrek, Nov. 19, 2026)

Chronicle’s read: Italy is a national yes, not an EU-wide one. A slipped committee date is still a meeting without a vote. The driverless Model Y is a stated plan from a live interview, not a filing or a timeline.

Around the ride

The same Thursday Dallas opened, Tesla Charging said Supercharger Waitlist was live at 300 U.S. sites. Drivers join a virtual queue in the app or on the car’s screen; non-Tesla EV drivers can join through the app. Two days earlier, Tesla opened its first Semi Megacharger in Texas, an eight-stall site in Mesquite, on the east side of Dallas. Electrek counted seven public Megacharger sites after the opening, against 37 sites Tesla had planned to open in 2026. (@TeslaCharging, Nov. 19, 2026; Electrek, Nov. 18, 2026)

Tesla Energy unveiled Powercore on November 17, a 7.4 MW solid-state unit combining a transformer, inverter and breaker, pitched for storage, solar and 800-volt data centers, where Tesla says it is working with Nvidia. First units are slated for 2027; Tesla named no price, customer or factory. The same week, The Boring Company said it had signed a 40-year agreement for a Music City Loop station at Nashville International Airport, 22 feet from the terminal, with rides to downtown in under 10 minutes, the company says. The station is not yet built or open. (@Tesla_Megapack, Nov. 17, 2026; @boringcompany, Nov. 19, 2026)

The wider companies

Starlink spent the same stretch arguing it can be ordinary infrastructure. On November 23, @Starlink said U.S. preorders for Mini 2, introduced November 10, are open by invitation. Starlink says the dish runs up to 4.5 hours on a battery or USB-C, has built-in Wi-Fi, and connects in 60 seconds. The post stated no ship date or price. That same day, NTIA announced a BEAD model agreement under which SpaceX will serve its awarded Starlink locations without taking the subsidy, while still meeting speed, equipment and low-cost-plan terms. State adoption is pending. Reported forgone amounts of about $738 million (Teslarati, Quartz) and about $636 million (Telecompetitor) are not stated by NTIA and are unconfirmed. (@Starlink, Nov. 10, 2026; NTIA, Nov. 23, 2026; Teslarati, Nov. 25, 2026)

At DealBook, Musk also said SpaceX will start launching second-generation direct-to-cell Starlink satellites next year, with roughly 40 times current bandwidth, and has designed rooftop stations for indoor Starlink Mobile. He said “I think” microcell production starts next year, and gave no service launch date. On November 14, Ship 42 became the first Starship upper stage caught by the tower; the booster was lost after a Gulf abort. Texas issued an air permit November 16 for SpaceX’s planned $24 billion supercompute site and gas plant near Starbase, about 750 megawatts. (TeslaNorth, Nov. 19, 2026; SpaceX, Flight 15; POLITICO, Nov. 17, 2026)

X general counsel James Burnham announced November 24 that the High Court in London had entered judgment against two defendants X sued over alleged Creator Revenue Sharing fraud. X alleged more than £207,000; the terms are not public. Chronicle rates the row medium confidence. The Financial Times reported a possible year-end Tesla–SpaceX merger deadline, though people familiar said an agreement is not close. This is reported, not confirmed. There is no agreement or filing. At DealBook, Musk said the companies would “probably” be better together, and that a merger “may or may not succeed.” (@BurnhamDC, Nov. 24, 2026; Electrek, citing the Financial Times, Nov. 24, 2026)

Chronicle’s read: The week is easiest to misread as a single arrival. Dallas is open. v15 is in a first wave, with a wide release Musk has dated to next month. Italy has approved a supervised system, and Brussels has not voted. The $299 subscription is required on invited personal orders, and no customer-owned Cybercab has been logged as delivered. What ties it together is the direction: from demo toward a paid, regulated service — and clocks that have not all run out.
