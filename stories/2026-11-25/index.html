Cybercab opens to the public in Dallas as Tesla turns autonomy into a service

Public two-seat rides began in a second city. FSD v15 started its first wave, aimed at a December wide release. Personal Cybercab orders carry a required $299 monthly subscription. Italy said yes; the EU meeting slipped to December 15.

In brief

@robotaxi · Nov. 19

“Howdy Dallas. Cybercab has arrived in your city.” Public rides opened through the Robotaxi app, the second city after Austin.

@Tesla · Nov. 21

FSD v15 began rolling out to a first wave. Musk said the wide release is next month. The 25x safety figure is Tesla’s own claim.

@teslaeurope · Nov. 20

Italy approved Tesla Assisted Driving, the 12th EU country and the first large market. The EU committee meeting moved to December 15, with no vote yet.

TeslaNorth, Texas DMV · Nov. 24

The authorized Texas Robotaxi list showed 822 Cybercabs and 734 Model Ys. The registry is not a ridership count.

What’s still open

Not a delivery
Personal Cybercab orders are invite-only, at $29,990 plus a required $299 monthly Autonomy Subscription. No customer-owned car had been logged as delivered as of November 16.

Unconfirmed
The Financial Times reported a possible year-end merger deadline. Neither Tesla nor SpaceX has agreed terms or filed a deal.

Pending
Starlink Mini 2 is on invitation-only U.S. preorder, with no public price or ship date. SpaceX’s BEAD subsidy figures are reported, not confirmed by NTIA.

Terms hidden
A UK High Court judgment for X was announced from the first page of an order. The terms are not public.
