Tesla says 300+ Cybercabs serving Austin one month after launch Open in the timeline

On the Q3 2026 call Elluswamy said Cybercab launches in Dallas “next month”.

Sat, Oct 10AutonomyTeslaPartly delivered

On October 10, 2026, Tesla’s Robotaxi account posted that one month after launch it had scaled to 300+ Cybercabs serving Austin—an 8× increase in 30 days—the first official public serving-Austin count at that scale. Tesla AI software head Ashok Elluswamy quote-posted that Cybercabs were everywhere in Austin and said “Dallas soon,” naming the next Cybercab city without a date. Independent trackers and Texas registry counts differed from Tesla’s serving figure; Tesla did not say how many of the 300+ run unsupervised or with a safety monitor. Commercial Austin Cybercab service began September 3 with open app hailing from September 10; NHTSA AQ26002 / Special Order and new defect petition DP26010 remained open in parallel.

NHTSA opens DP26010 on Robotaxi school-zone speeding petition Open in the timeline

Fri, Oct 9LegalTeslaPending · not a promise

On October 9, 2026, NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation opened Defect Petition DP26010 after receiving a September 23 petition asking it to investigate model-year 2026 Tesla Robotaxis (Model Y and Cybercab) with the Automated Driving System engaged. The petitioner alleges the ADS exceeds posted speed limits in active school zones and supplied three video links. ODI estimates about 1,000 affected vehicles and records four incidents (one ODI complaint and three social-media reports in the petition), with no crashes, injuries, or fatalities. Opening the petition means ODI will decide whether to grant or deny a formal investigation—it is not itself a recall or Preliminary Evaluation. The petition is filed under ODI number 11766676. Cybercab makes European public debut at Paris Motor Show Open in the timeline

Mon, Oct 12AutonomyTesla

On October 12, 2026 (Mondial de l’Auto press day), Tesla displayed a gold production Cybercab from its U.S. Robotaxi fleet on stand C25 in Hall 7.1 at Paris Expo Porte de Versailles—the vehicle’s first public European appearance. Show director Serge Gachot had told Bloomberg the unit would be walk-around only (no steering wheel or pedals; no rides). Tesla-Mag and CyberCab.News documented the press-day reveal with Optimus Gen 2 nearby; public days run October 13–18. Separate free Full Self-Driving (Supervised) test drives use Model 3s. No European Cybercab ride service or launch date was announced.
