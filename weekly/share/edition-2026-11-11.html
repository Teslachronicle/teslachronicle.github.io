Musk: Cybertruck production at Giga Texas winds down mid-2027 for Cybercab Open in the timeline

In a November 26, 2026 email, Tesla Taiwan told Cybertruck reservation holders that production would gradually end and their reservations would be cancelled automatically, with the NT$3,000 deposit returned and eight years of Premium Connectivity offered to those who order a Model 3 or Model Y and take delivery by March 31, 2027 (TeslaNorth and Drive Tesla Canada, citing News Pie and Electrify.tw).

Thu, Nov 5ProductTeslaPending · not a promise

On the evening of November 5, 2026 PT (06:30 UTC November 6), Elon Musk posted on X that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year to make room for increased Cybercab production. Cybertruck lead engineer Wes Morrill wrote that Cybertruck was a technology development platform that lives on through Cybercab; Musk later replied that the Cybertruck fleet will be around for a long time. Tesla did not say whether Cybertruck would move to another factory. A similar mainland China cancellation notice was shared on X but is not independently confirmed.

SEC finishes paying out the 2018 "funding secured" Fair Fund Open in the timeline

Tue, Nov 10LegalTesla

On November 10, 2026 the SEC told a Manhattan federal court it had finished distributing the $40 million Fair Fund built from the $20 million fines Musk and Tesla each paid to settle the 2018 "funding secured" case, and asked the judge to close it. A claims administrator paid $41.53 million, with interest and after fees, to 3,350 eligible claimants, covering 51.7% of their approved losses; 3,046 claims were rejected. FAA proposes streamlined commercial launch and re-entry rules Open in the timeline

Thu, Nov 5RegulatorySpaceXPending · not a promise

On November 5, 2026 the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration proposed streamlining its rules for commercial space launches and re-entries, including clearer thresholds for when a flight abort is mandatory and revised rules for vehicle breakup and falling-debris risk to people on the ground. The FAA said it plans to overhaul its space licensing framework next year and is expanding its Office of Commercial Space Transportation with a team of aerospace engineers to speed approvals. Reuters reported the changes would make launch approvals easier for SpaceX and Blue Origin; the White House goal is at least 1,000 launches and re-entries a year by 2030.
