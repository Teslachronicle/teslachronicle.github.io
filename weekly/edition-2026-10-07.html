Musk: AI5 memory halved to 72GB, AI6 cut a third to 144GB for Optimus volume

Thursday, October 1, 2026 · Robot · Tesla

On October 1, 2026, Elon Musk posted that Tesla cut the RAM on its AI5 chip in half, to 72GB of LPDDR5, and on AI6 by a third, to 144GB of LPDDR6, calling it the only way to get enough volume for Optimus production and saying it greatly reduces cost with what Tesla expects to be a negligible effect on Optimus performance. The post replied to a thread about humanoid robots needing about 200GB of RAM each, after Micron’s earnings call made that estimate. This is a chip spec change, not an Optimus production milestone.

Tesla launches Emergency Drive Away at U.S. Superchargers

Thursday, October 1, 2026 · Product · Tesla

On October 1, 2026, Tesla Charging announced Emergency Drive Away, a software feature that lets a driver shift into Drive and pull away while still plugged into a Supercharger. It needs software 2026.38.3, excludes pre-2021 Model S and Model X, and applies only to U.S. Superchargers for now. Tesla said use will damage both the car and the stall, that a charge-port service visit is mandatory, that Supercharger repair can cost up to $25,000, and that deliberate misuse brings penalties. Coverage tied the launch to the August 2026 shooting at a Twin Falls, Idaho Supercharger.

Powershare Home Backup comes to new Model 3 and Model Y with Powerwall 3

Tuesday, October 6, 2026 · Energy · Tesla

On October 6, 2026, Tesla Energy posted that Powershare Home Backup is now available for new Model 3 and Model Y with Powerwall 3, extending home backup by over two days with the vehicle; Tesla quoted it saying that with Powerwall 3 every Tesla can now serve as a home's backup battery. Before this, Cybertruck was the only Tesla with vehicle-to-home backup. Tesla's Powershare page guarantees the feature on new Model 3 and Model Y ordered in the U.S. and Puerto Rico on or after October 1, 2026; some earlier cars qualify if the touchscreen shows Powershare Support enabled. Powerwall 2 and Powerwall+ support is listed as coming soon, and grid export (V2G) stays Cybertruck-only.

First East Coast Megacharger opens in Georgia, linking Atlanta and Savannah for Tesla Semi

Wednesday, October 7, 2026 · Charging · Tesla

On October 7, 2026, Tesla Charging listed a new 8-stall Tesla Megacharger in Conley, Georgia, on Atlanta's southeast edge, and the Tesla Semi account called it the first Megacharger on the East Coast. On October 8, a 6-stall Megacharger opened at Pilot #1390 in Ellabell, near Savannah, the first site from the Pilot and Tesla partnership announced in January 2026, which had targeted first sites for summer. Tesla Semi then posted that new Megacharger sites between Atlanta and Savannah are online, with many more Southeast sites coming soon. Stalls are rated up to 1.2 MW. This gives Semi its first public charging corridor in the Southeast, two weeks after Nevada high-volume Semi production launched.
